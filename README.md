# Sunset City

An open-world street racer made with Three.js. Drive a mid-engined supercar through a California-style city of boulevards, palms, shops, apartments and glass towers, then out along motorways into a 12 × 16 km world: a coastline, snow-capped mountains, desert and canyons, grassland, a dry-lake airfield and two secret military bunkers you can drive into. It's lit at golden hour by default, with the sun low and backlit through the haze. It's a browser game with generated models and textures, so it aims for the *mood* of a modern open-world racer rather than matching a big studio's assets. It's a sister game to [MushtaqTrack](https://github.com/rauf-mushtaq/mushtaqtrack) and reuses what that game learned about car handling, iPad performance, and touch, gamepad and steering-wheel controls.

## Native version (C++ and HLSL)

A native Windows version of this game, written in C++ with Direct3D 12 and HLSL shaders, is in [`native/`](native/): the world, a detailed wet city with reflections, traffic, pedestrians, races, and an escape-the-police mode with a gadget car (see its README). It has more than the web version in some ways (the police mode, reflections) and less in others (bunkers, map, sound). The web version below stays as it is until the native one has been tested on real hardware.

## Unreal Engine 5 version (the "file version")

An Unreal Engine 5 project of the same game is in [`unreal/`](unreal/) (see its README): the city made block by block as you move, people (about 1 in 11 armed, 1 in 16 a big muscular man who fights back), the police, rooms you can walk into and rob, and cars, lit by Lumen with hardware ray tracing, virtual shadow maps and a physical sky, with physically based materials. **It has not been compiled or run** (there is no Unreal Engine where it was written): the engine-independent core is tested (105 checks) and the engine-facing code was syntax-checked against a stand-in for Unreal's API, so expect a few compile errors on the first build, and a picture that is a generator's foundation, not GTA 6.

## Play

The game loads its modules from files, so it has to be served over HTTP, not opened as a file:

- **GitHub Pages:** in this repository go to Settings → Pages → Deploy from a branch → `main`, folder `/`. The game is then at `https://rauf-mushtaq.github.io/Racing/`.
- **On your computer:** run `python3 -m http.server` in this folder and open `http://localhost:8000`.

There's nothing to build or install, and **nothing is fetched from another site**: three.js and the fonts are in `vendor/` and `assets/fonts/`, the models are in `assets/`, and every texture and sound is generated when the game starts. After the first load a **service worker** (`sw.js`) keeps every file, so the game **plays offline** (on an iPad: open it in Safari, then Share → Add to Home Screen for a full-screen app with its own icon). `?nosw` in the address turns the offline cache off.

## What's in it

- **On foot:** you are a person, not just a car. **Free Roam starts on the pavement next to your parked Vento SV.** Walk (**W A S D**, or the left stick, or the touch stick), **Shift** to sprint, **Space** to jump, **Z** or **Ctrl** to crouch, and look round with the mouse (click the game to capture it; Esc lets go), the right stick, a drag on the right half of a touchscreen, or **J L I K**. The camera swings round behind you as you run. Your character is **a real person** (see below: six photo-textured people to choose from in the Garage) with a walk, jog, sprint, crouch, jump, idle and knocked-flat animation, and footsteps that change with the ground. **You can carry guns** (next bullet). Walk up to your car and press **F** to get in (the camera glides round into the driving view); press **F** again when you have stopped to get out. You cannot walk through buildings, poles, parked cars or traffic, and a car that hits you knocks you flat.
- **Guns on foot:** press **1 2 3 4** (or the **GUN** button) for a **silenced pistol**, a **submachine gun**, a **pump shotgun** or a **shoulder-fired rocket launcher**; the same number again (or **0**) puts it away, and pulling the trigger with nothing in your hands draws the last one. **Click** (the first click captures the mouse) or **X** fires, **right click** or **V** holds the gun up and **aims**: the camera slides in over your shoulder, a crosshair appears where the shots will go, and the character turns to face it, chest and head following, both arms reaching for the gun (the arms are solved properly, shoulder to elbow to hand, so each gun sits in the hands the way it would in life: low across the belt when you are just carrying it, up and extended when you aim). **R** reloads (you can see the reload: the gun tips and the other hand drops), **G** throws a **grenade** (it bounces, then goes off after two seconds). Bullets are real rays: they hit buildings, the ground, police cars, traffic and parked cars, with tracers, sparks and muzzle flash; shotgun pellets spread and lose strength with distance; rockets fly, trail smoke and blow up on contact, throwing cars into the air. Police cars have hit points (a few magazines, or one rocket), wrecked ones burn, and bystander cars can be wrecked too. **Firing a gun in the street brings the police** ("SHOTS FIRED"), and a gunfight keeps the heat up. Gunfire scares the pedestrians. Walk back to the car and the boot restocks all your guns. On a tablet: **FIRE** (it draws a gun if you have none out), **AIM**, **GUN**, **RELOAD** and **NADE**, with a little help from aim assist toward cars near the crosshair. On a gamepad: RT fires, LB aims, X reloads, Y changes gun, R3 throws a grenade.
- **Police officers on foot:** when you are on foot and a police car gets within about 35 metres and can see you, it **pulls up and two officers step out** (a SWAT pair from a pursuit truck). They are real people too (a blue uniform, a black one with a cap, a plain-clothes detective in a POLICE vest, and SWAT in goggles and a balaclava) with a pistol or a submachine gun held two-handed. Most **hold their ground and shoot**: they stand, crouch and sidestep about 10 to 25 metres away and fire bursts, missing more the further away you are, the faster you run and the lower you crouch (and a little at first). Some **rush you**, firing as they come, and **arrest you if one gets right beside you and you stay put**. They back off if you get too close, go to where they last saw you if you break line of sight (smoke screens and corners work), and steer round buildings. They **run dry and reload** (crouching, the gun tipped and the other hand reaching for a magazine, and they cannot fire meanwhile). They **call for backup** on the radio (a hand to the shoulder while they stand still and open to a shot: kill the one with the radio and no call goes out) when they first find you, when one is badly hurt and when a colleague is shot beside them; it brings one or two more cars (a pursuit truck at three stars and up) and a little more heat. And they **vault** over cars, bins, benches, hydrants and road barriers in their way (a hop over the top) instead of going round. **You can shoot them** (the pistol takes four shots, a shotgun blast at close range one, a shot to the head does more than double, rockets and grenades throw them across the road); a downed officer lies where he fell, and killing police turns the heat up. When the last officer of a car is down (or has gone back to it) that car drives on, and **if you get back in your car the officers run back to theirs and the chase is on again**. Their bullets take your armour (the bar at the top), which slowly comes back if you are left alone for a few seconds. How many officers can be out at once depends on the Graphics setting (8 on Ultra down to 3 on Low).
- **Cover, drops and the helicopter:** officers who are hurt or reloading **run to the nearest car and crouch behind it**, standing up to fire in short bursts. **Cars and walls stop bullets, yours and theirs** (a crouch behind a car is real cover). Bullets leave **holes in walls, the ground and cars** and **cracks in glass**. A dead officer **drops ammunition** (now and then body armour) that you pick up by walking over it, and the car's boot only restocks you **once every 90 seconds**. From **three stars** a **police helicopter** circles you with a searchlight and keeps you spotted while it can see you; shoot it down (bullets, rockets, a blast) and the next one takes about 45 seconds. The arrest has a **progress bar** (and a warning): walking or standing still beside an officer fills it in about 3.4 seconds; a **jog or a sprint breaks away**. At three stars and up, when you drive fast along a city street, **cruisers also park across the street ahead as a roadblock**.
- **Weak guns on the police cars:** the cruisers (and, a bit harder, the pursuit trucks) fire short bursts from their roofs at you, in a car or on foot, whenever they can see you and are within about 45 metres ("THE POLICE ARE SHOOTING"). They are **poor shots** (worse the further you are and the faster you go), each hit takes only a point or two of armour (your armour plate cuts that to a third), and only a few cars (two plus the number of stars) fire at once. **They still ram you**, and ramming hurts far more.
- **Gadgets in your own car:** when the police are after you in **Free Roam**, your Ferrari has the same hidden workshop as the chase car: **machine guns** slide out of the nose, a **homing missile**, **proximity bombs**, an **oil slick**, a **smoke screen** and an **armour plate** that rises behind the cabin (with an energy bubble) are on **J K L O I H** (or 1 to 6), on a gamepad, or on the on-screen buttons. Get away (or leave the car, and the guns go with the car) and they are put away.
- **Pedestrians:** the pavements have people on them, walking round the blocks, stopping for a moment, stepping aside for you. They run when something loud happens (a crash, an explosion, a crime nearby, a car racing past) and a car that hits one knocks them over. They share the character's skeleton and animation, are dyed in different clothes, skin tones and hair, and are drawn as instanced meshes, about a dozen draw calls for the whole crowd. **The people nearest the camera are real people** (eight more Rocketbox adults, with recorded walking and blinking), handed out from a small pool (3 to 10, by graphics tier) to whoever is closest and given back as they walk away; the rest of the crowd is the instanced figures.
- **Trouble:** in Free Roam, **hit a car, knock someone down or smash something and the police come.** Street furniture breaks for real: lamp posts and poles topple about their feet and spark, hydrants fly off and leave a jet of water and a spreading puddle, bins and benches are flung down the street, and they stay broken (even when the street streams back in). Parked cars can be shoved about too. The first crime brings a one-star squad; more crimes turn the heat up. You can drive off, or leave the car and run: on foot you are seen from much less far (and from half as far again crouching), but a cruiser that spots you pulls up and its officers get out (see below), and one of them getting right beside you for a couple of seconds arrests you.
- **The car:** the Vento SV, a mid-engined, all-wheel-drive supercar (1,650 kg, 690 Nm, a six-speed gearbox, 8,000 rpm red line: these are the game's own numbers) with a big rear wing and a centre exhaust that shoots a blue nitro flame. Its **body is a 3D model of a real V8 sports coupe** (a Ferrari 458 Italia, see the credits) with the maker's yellow shield badges and wheel and steering-wheel centres turned to dark metal; the engine, grip and handling are the game's. It has nine paint colours in the Garage. **Your character sits at the wheel** in the chase views (hands on the wheel, head into the bend, feet on the pedals), in this car and in the grand tourer.
- **A big city:** 25 × 25 roads, about 4.4 km across (nine times the area of the first version), streamed in around you as you drive so it never stops to load. Every fourth road is a six-lane boulevard with a palm-planted median and overhead signs. From the middle outwards: **downtown** towers (round, chamfered and rounded-corner towers with stepped tiers, stone plinths, tapered crowns and masts), **mid-rise** blocks of glass, offices, brick walk-ups and shops, **low-rise** neighbourhoods, and **suburbs** of detached houses with tiled hip roofs, lawns, driveways and hedges. There are parks with paths, car parks, billboards, traffic lights at every junction, power poles with wires, and hydrants, bins and benches along the kerbs. Street corners have rounded kerbs.
- **A river, bridges, roundabouts and landmarks (the city is not one repeated crossing any more):** **Sunset River** winds about 2 km east through the north of downtown, from **Sunset Lake** (with a lighthouse on an island) to the **East Basin**, between stone quays with a railing and lamps, and the ground really is cut away there (every road and pavement material discards the pixels over the water, from a small distance-field texture, so the banks are clean and the water is below the road). It is crossed by **twelve bridges of seven different designs**: a **suspension** bridge with orange towers on the central boulevard, a **cable-stayed** one, a **gate-towered** bridge, three **stone arches** (grey, sandstone, brick) that hump up in the middle, two green and rust **through-trusses**, two **Ponte-Vecchio-style** bridges with a street of little shops built along each side, and plain concrete girder bridges; each has its own deck with markings, kerbs, pavements and lamps, and the car, traffic, people and police use the deck (up the hump and down). You cannot drive or walk into the water. **Boats** ply the river between the bridges and others are moored at the quays; the minimap and big map show the water. About one junction in eight is a **roundabout** (a kerbed island with a fountain, an obelisk, a wood or a statue; traffic goes round it counter-clockwise on smooth curves, there are no lights, and you cannot drive over the island). And there are **landmarks** that give districts a character: **Sunset Tower** (240 m, the tallest thing), a **stadium** with floodlights and a pitch, a **cathedral** with twin spires and a square with a fountain and the **old town** round it (tiled roofs, brick and plaster), a **big wheel** that turns, and the **docks** along the east end of the river (warehouses, container stacks and cranes).
- **Street life, banks and a real skyline:** **street performers** stand at about fifty places (the pavement by where you start, the cathedral square, the plazas, the foot of the bridges, corners all over the city): a **guitarist** picking chords, a **saxophonist**, a **bucket drummer**, a **juggler** with three balls in the air and a **living statue** painted silver on a plinth with a hat in front. They are real people (the same models as the pedestrians), they play real-time synthesised music from where they stand (it is panned and fades with distance), and some of the pedestrians **stop in a half circle to watch, clap when a piece ends and scatter if something explodes**. Only the nearest three are real at a time, so it costs nothing elsewhere. The buildings have more character too: **banks** (stone, a portico of six columns under a pediment, steps, bronze doors, the name in gold and a cash machine on the pavement), **hotels** (a tall tower with a canopy and flags) and **cinemas** (a lit marquee and posters), **café terraces** with tables, chairs and striped umbrellas on the pavement, **plazas** instead of some blocks (a fountain, a clock tower, a bandstand, market stalls, planters, rows of trees), and **building sites** with a tall crane. Downtown is taller, and **fourteen blocks hold a skyscraper of their own shape**: an art-deco crown, a twisting tower, a tower with a glass pyramid on top, twin towers joined by a sky bridge, a drum with a ring round it, a leaning tower, a gateway of two slabs, a needle, a stepped tower with a garden on every roof and a blade, besides Sunset Tower at 240 m.
- **Hills, and roads that are not on the grid:** the ground is not flat any more. **Four hills** (Sunset Heights at 56 m, Mesa Park, Observatory Hill and South Ridge) and gentle swells over the suburbs lift the outer city, while the river, downtown and the landmarks stay level. Every road, pavement, kerb, lawn, building, lamp, tree and parked car is laid on the ground (a building stands level on its lot, its walls carried down to the lowest ground under it), traffic and people follow it, the car climbs it (the steepest road is one in seven), and the big map is shaded to show it. The grid is broken up too: **two long diagonal boulevards** (the Grand Diagonal, 4.2 km, and the Cross Diagonal) cut across it at 45 degrees through the middle of the blocks, so the blocks become pentagons and triangles and the buildings **wedges** (and little parks where a wedge is too small), with double yellow lines, dashed lanes, lamps and plane trees, and their own traffic; and **two roads spiral up the hills** (Skyline Drive and Mesa Drive). They are made by cutting the pavement away (the same mask trick as the river) and the cruisers can use them.
- **A city that is not a grid:** on top of the hills and the two diagonal boulevards there is now a web of curved roads (`src/avenues.js`): **round the cathedral, the Old Town** has three rings (Cathedral Ring, Market Ring, Outer Ring) and seven narrow lanes running out from its square, so its blocks are wedges and slivers; **two rings round the stadium**; and a **terrace road round every hill**, or two, like a contour (the hill terraces turn the lots they pass into lawn). **Eleven more roads** (ten crescents, sweeps and rings: Northgate Crescent, Grand Crescent, Compass Ring, Eastbank Curve, Meridian Sweep, Stadium Sweep, Westfield Sweep, Sunset Circle (a ring round the junction just south of where you start), Plaza Crescent, Riverside Sweep; and the Orchard Diagonal) were added to cut up the plain blocks that were left, and the blocks they cut become wedges and slivers too. 34 roads in all, 60 km of them, every one kept clear of the river, the landmarks, the skyscraper blocks, the plazas and all junctions (tested). Where two of them cross, the junction is left open (no kerb, no lines); there are no traffic lights there, so cars from the two roads can pass through each other on the rare occasion they meet. The lots they cut are filled with wedge-shaped buildings or little pocket parks. Plazas had to move out of their way, so there are fewer of them near the middle.
- **Fewer repeated squares:** a plain block is one that no road but the grid touches (not a park, plaza, lake, landmark or skyscraper block). There were **307** of them across the city and **23** within 900 m of where you start; the new roads bring those to **193** (37% fewer) and **9** (61% fewer), and `tests/map.mjs` counts them and fails if they creep back above 28% fewer. (Counting only blocks that a road really runs through, not one that just grazes an edge, it is 310 down to 202 and 25 down to 12.) (This changes the layout, the shape of the blocks and of the buildings on them; the buildings in a block that is still plain are still rectangles.)
- **Places outside the city** (`src/settlements.js`, `towns.js`, `sites-*.js`), one or more in every kind of country, each on level ground, joined to the motorway by its own road and drawn on the big map with its name:
  - grassland: **Willow Creek**, a town with a main street of shops, a church, a town hall with a clock, a water tower, a diner, a petrol station, a park and houses (built with the city's own building generators), and three farms (**Hollow Farm**, **Meadow Farm**, **Windy Acres**: farmhouse, barn, silos, a turning windmill, tractor, hay, fences and fields of crops in rows)
  - the coast: **Port Meridian**, a working harbour (quay, warehouses, two gantry cranes, container stacks, fuel tanks, a pier with a lighthouse, fishing boats and a cargo ship at anchor), **Sandy Cove**, a seaside village (colourful houses, shops, boardwalk, a pier, palms, umbrellas, a lifeguard tower), and **North Point Light**, a lighthouse with a turning beam at night
  - the mountains: **Frostline Resort**, with a timber lodge, a hotel, chalets with snow on the roofs, a car park, an ice rink and a chairlift up the slope
  - the desert: **Dusty Gulch**, a ghost town (a saloon, hotel, bank, sheriff, boardwalks, a church, water tower and a mine), the **Black Mesa Oil Field** (pumpjacks nodding, tanks, a flaring stack), the **Dry Lake Solar** farm (about 480 panels, a substation and a pylon) and the **Dry Lake Telescope** (six turning dishes and a mast with a red light)
  - A car can drive down every street (tested); the buildings are solid; the parked cars in the towns are the same ones the city has. There are no pedestrians or moving traffic out there yet.
- **Cars drawn like a modern engine draws them** (`src/cardetail.js`, `src/fleet.js`, `src/carModel.js`; the police cars and all the traffic): every kind of car has **two levels of detail** and each car in the street is drawn at the one its distance calls for (55 m), the way an engine with a streaming and level-of-detail system swaps a far model for a near one. **Far:** the body with its glass painted on, drum wheels and lamps (three draw calls for a whole street of them). **Near:** the body without painted glass; **real glass** (smooth, dark, with the street reflected in it through the same screen-space ray tracing as the road) over a **driver's seat, passenger seat, dashboard, rear bench and steering wheel**; mirrors, door handles and shut lines, a grille with slats, bumpers, plates; and **wheels as separate parts** (a tyre with a tread normal map, a steel rim with spokes, a brake disc and a calliper) that **turn with the speed and the front pair steer with the car's turning**. A police car also has a **push bar**, a **light bar housing** (the lights flash red and blue as before), spotlights on the door pillars, an aerial, a **blue line along the doors and POLICE on both doors and the bonnet**, a laptop glowing in the cabin. The materials are physically based: paint is a metallic base with a clear coat and a sheen, the glass and paint take the screen-space reflections, the rims are metal, the tyres rubber, the lamps emissive (they bloom).
- **Fists, boots and a knife** (`src/melee.js`): with nothing in your hands **X** (or click, or the FIRE button) throws a **jab, then a cross**, one after the other for as long as you keep pressing; **Z** (or **B** on a pad, or the **KICK** button) is a **kick** that reaches further and can catch two people; **5** draws a **knife** (**slash, stab, slash**). Each blow starts, swings (the arms, chest and legs are posed for it: the fist is thrown out to the end of the arm with the chest turning into it, the kick leans you back, the knife is really in the hand), and lands at one moment in the middle of the swing on whoever is in front within reach and arc: pedestrians go down and the crowd scatters, officers lose health, shopkeepers and guards go down, glass cases break. One punch on a stranger is a scuffle nobody reports; a second within half a minute, or any blade, is **assault** and the police come.
- **A cinematic picture** (`src/clouds.js`, `src/motes.js`, `src/birds.js`, `src/dof.js`, the grade in `src/main.js`): the sky has **real shaded clouds** (a shader finds where each view ray meets a deck of cloud and lights it the way a cloud is lit: bright on the sun side, blue-grey underneath, a silver rim when you look at the sun, thinning into the haze at the horizon, drifting with the wind) instead of flat sprites; **dust and pollen** hang in the air and glitter when you look towards the low sun; **flocks of birds** circle as dark silhouettes against the sky; on Ultra and High the lens has **depth of field** (it focuses on you or your car, the far streets soften, bright lights out of focus become soft discs); the grade **cools the shadows and warms the highlights**, brings dull colours up more than vivid ones, and at sunset and night bright lights pull a long blue **anamorphic streak**; the bloom swells when you look into the sun. The ground and walls are **weathered**: oil and damp stains on the road and pavement (and the odd chewing-gum spot), different ages of tarmac, rain streaks running down the buildings from the ledges, and in rain the puddles are pocked with **rain rings**. **Flowering shrubs** (hot pink, red, orange, violet, white) are in the beds and medians, the bushes are denser (no dark core showing), and the palm fronds are solid leaflets that no longer break into dots in the distance.
- **People who fight back** (`src/brawl.js`): about 1 in 11 of the people on the pavements is **armed** (a pistol under the jacket) and about 1 in 16 is a **big, muscular man** (broad chest and shoulders, thick arms and neck: the real model's bones are scaled for it). Leave them alone and they are just pedestrians. **Hit one** (a punch, a kick, the knife, a shot, a car) and he becomes a fighter, a real person who goes for you: the **big man charges and hits hard** (a jab, a cross, and now and then a slow haymaker, with a long wind-up you can step back from, that knocks you flat) and takes a lot of punishment; the **armed one draws a pistol and shoots at you from a distance** (burst after burst, missing more the further away and the faster you move; he runs when he is badly hurt); now and then one is both (big *and* armed: he shoots as he comes, then hits). Armed people near a gunshot may join in; a plain citizen who is hit just goes down. Your punches, kicks and knife hurt them, and so do bullets (bullets now hit people on the pavement) and explosions. A beaten fighter is **knocked out** (lies still for a few seconds, then gets up and slopes off), one who was shot or cut is down for good, and you take what is in his **wallet** ($40 to $520). Shots in the street bring the police like any other gunfire. You now have **health** in a fight (the bar under the money; it comes back slowly), and with none left you are **knocked out**: you wake beside your car, minus a hospital bill.
- **Buildings you can walk into, and rob** (`src/venues.js`, `src/interior.js`, `src/rooms.js`, `src/heist.js`): walk up to a door of a **petrol station, convenience store, diamond jeweller, bank or museum** (the signs say which; the prompt says ENTER) and press **F** (or the button). The room is built when you go in and put high above the street, straight over the door; the camera then draws only that room (the city is not drawn at all) with its own lights and reflections, so a room costs less than the street. The people in it are real people (the same models and animation as everyone else). **Draw a weapon and staff put their hands up and customers run or crouch; keep the gun on staff or they will creep to the alarm button, knock them out and they cannot.** Things to take (a till, a drawer, a safe, a glass case, a vault, a stack of cash, a treasure) are done with the use button beside them and go on while you stay close. What is taken goes in your **bag**; it is only **banked** when you are away from the police (lose them, or leave with no alarm raised), and lost if you are busted or killed. Money is kept in your browser (`$` at the top).
  - **They get harder as they get richer**: a **petrol station** is the easiest (a till and one unarmed clerk, a slow silent alarm, one star, about $300 to $900); a **convenience store** adds a safe in the back (the clerk watches the till until threatened); a **jeweller** has six glass cases (smash them: the alarm), an armed guard who fights, two cameras, police in six seconds, two stars; a **bank** has three tellers, two armed guards, cameras, a manager with a **key card** (hands up, then take it) and a **vault** behind a 26 second drill (loud) or a 3 second card swipe, police in three seconds, three stars, $50,000 to $140,000; the **museum** has three armed guards **patrolling**, three sweeping cameras, **laser beams** (three heights, a fence round each treasure) and a **security room** whose panel switches them all off for 55 seconds, treasures worth $315,000 together, police at once, four stars.
  - Guards shoot straight at you (worse the further you are, the faster you run and the lower you crouch), and what they hit takes your armour. With the police outside and you in, they **cannot see you or arrest you through the wall, and do not give up while you are in there**: leave through them or fight.
- **The big map and teleporting** (Tab, M or the MAP button; `src/bigmap.js`): the whole world or the city close up. **Drag** to move, **pinch** (or the **+** and **−** buttons, or the wheel) to zoom, **CITY** and **WORLD** jump between the two views, **ME** comes back to where you are, and **PLACES** lists the city's districts, the hills and every town, farm and site by name. It is drawn live from the game's own numbers (every block with its buildings in the district's colour, every road and ring and lane, the river and lakes, the hills shaded, the coast and the land smooth to the pixel), so it stays sharp at any zoom, with names that appear as you zoom in, a scale bar and a compass. **Tap anywhere** and a card says what is there and how far away it is, with **SET WAYPOINT** and **TELEPORT HERE**. A teleport asks twice (the first tap only arms it, so a stray tap cannot throw you across the world), puts the car in a lane of the nearest road (the city's streets and winding roads, the motorways, the streets of the towns) facing the way you were facing, or where you tapped if you are out in the open country with no road near, never in water or inside a building, and if you were on foot you stand beside the car. It is switched off during a race, a police chase and the performance test (the button is greyed out and says why). `tests/bigmap.mjs` drives all of it, including a teleport to every named place.
- **The wider world:** about 12 × 16 km, streamed in as you drive. **Coast** to the west (beach, rippling sea, palm trees), **snowy mountains** to the north (pine forests, snow-laden pines, white peaks and a ski lodge), **desert** to the east (dunes, saguaro cacti, boulders, red sandstone mesas and a canyon with bridges over it, and a roadside gas station), and **grassland** to the south (trees, scrub and a wind farm). Cliffs are textured from the side, and desert rock is banded like sandstone.
- **Motorways:** a 43 km ring motorway plus four spokes out of the city, with lane markings, a concrete median barrier, steel guardrails (solid to the car), bridge parapets and piers. The roads are graded to climb passes and cross the canyon, and the valley is carved around them. Motorway traffic includes box trucks and buses, in both directions.
- **Secret places:** two hidden bunkers, **Site 7** under a snowy mountain and **Site 12** in a red mesa. Each has a hazard-striped apron, a restricted-area sign, blast doors, a tunnel and a lit hangar with crates, drums, a jet and a truck, and a hidden service road leading to it. There's also the **Dry Lake Airfield** on a flat lakebed, with a runway you can drive down, hangars, a control tower, radar, fuel tanks, jets and a fenced perimeter with a gate. Drive up to one to discover it. It then shows on the map, and your discoveries are saved.
- **Handling:** a four-wheel simulation, tuned to be easy to pick up: the keyboard and touch controls turn the wheel and apply the pedals progressively (like a real wheel and pedals, not on/off), the steering lock shrinks with speed, and a real steering wheel is left unfiltered. Each wheel has its own suspension (spring, damper, bump stop and anti-roll bars), so the body dives under braking, squats under power, rolls in corners and rocks over kerbs, and each tyre's grip comes from its own load. Each wheel spins on its own through an all-wheel-drive drivetrain (30/70 front/rear) with limited-slip differentials, so wheelspin and lock-ups are real. The tyres share their grip between braking or accelerating and cornering. Steering uses Ackermann geometry. The car does 0–100 km/h in about 3.3 seconds, has a top speed of about 340 km/h, and stops from 100 km/h in about 34 m. ABS works wheel by wheel, traction control trims the engine when a wheel spins up, and stability control (ESP) is there too. Each one lights up on the dial when it's acting; turn them off for wheelspin, locked brakes and slides. The gearbox is automatic or manual, and the handbrake starts drifts.
- **Traffic:** saloons, SUVs, hatchbacks and vans drive on the right in their lanes. They stop at red lights, follow each other, slow for turns, wait at junctions and brake for you, and if you hit one it gets knocked aside (and settles onto the slope it ends up on, instead of sinking into a hill or floating over a dip). Traffic stays around you wherever you go.
- **Police chase:** a mode (menu → **Police Chase**) where the police are after you and you drive a silver 1960s-style grand tourer with a few extras from the workshop. There are **a lot of police now**: a one-star squad is six cruisers and five stars is a small army of up to 26 (heavy pursuit trucks join at four). They chase you along the streets, search for you when they lose sight, ram you and call for more as the heat climbs. **To get away you must stay out of sight for a whole minute**; being seen for more than half a second starts the minute again, and a big countdown shows how long you have left. You can jump out and hide on foot. Get wrecked, or boxed in and stopped, and it's over. The same pursuit (in your own Ferrari, with the same gadgets) starts in Free Roam when you commit a crime, and when you shake them off the game carries on. You have **machine guns** that slide out of the wings (they overheat), a **homing missile**, **proximity bombs**, an **oil slick**, a **smoke screen** (cops can't see through it), an **armour shield** and nitro. Ammo slowly refills. Explosions throw cars into the air and leave burning wrecks, and bystander cars get wrecked too (which turns the heat up). The gadget buttons are on screen on a tablet; on a keyboard they are J K L O I H (or 1 to 6); on a gamepad B (guns), RB (missile), LB (bomb), D-pad up (oil), D-pad down (smoke) and right-stick click (armour). **Chase music:** on the chase screen (or in Settings) you can pick a song file from your own device, the James Bond theme if you own it, and it plays during the chase. It is kept in your browser on that device only: the game does not include any song and nothing is uploaded.
- **Events:** fifteen races against the clock through checkpoint gates, with gold, silver and bronze medals and your best times saved. Nine are in the city, from the 4 km Boulevard Dash to the 10 km Grand Tour. Six are open country: Desert Highway, Mountain Pass, Coast Highway, Southern Freeway, Runway Run (down the airfield's runway) and the full 43 km Grand Ring. **Free Roam** has no clock.
- **The map:** a minimap in the corner (it shows the motorways once you leave the city), and the big map (Tab, M or the MAP button; see above) where you tap a spot to set a waypoint or teleport there. An arrow and a distance point you to the waypoint, and it's marked on the minimap.
- **HUD:** a round rev counter and speedometer with the gear in the middle, the ABS/ESP/TCS+ lights and a nitro gauge, a heading-up minimap, a checkpoint arrow and a race timer.
- **Cameras:** wide chase (the default), close chase, **driver view** (from the seat: a leather, stitched-rim wheel that turns with yours, gloved hands and sleeved arms on it, live rev and speed gauges, a dashboard, seats, door cards, a mirror, and a head that moves with the braking, acceleration and cornering forces, looks into the bend and shakes a little with the engine and the road), hood and bumper. Press C, the ◉ camera button, or ◉ CAM on touch. On a PC, drag the mouse to look around the car and use the wheel to zoom. Hold B to look back.
- **Time of day:** **Golden Hour** (the default: low backlit sun, long shadows, light shafts and a warm film-style grade), a bright **Day**, **Sunset** with a pink smoke plume, or **Night** (Settings → Time of day). Night has a moon with a halo, a field of stars, windows lit in every tower, glowing signs, street lamps pooling light on the pavement, wet shiny roads that catch the lights and neon, and headlight beams on the road in front of every car (yours, the traffic and the police). Dusk gets the beams too.
- **Rain** (Settings → Rain, at any time of day, takes effect at once): streaks falling past the camera, wet roads and pavements, thicker haze and the sound of it. Rain at night is the best way to see the city.

## Keeping the frame rate up

The resolution already scales itself (60% to 100%) to hold the frame rate. On top of that a **performance guard** (`src/perf.js`) watches the frame time, works out whether the graphics or the JavaScript is the limit, and steps things down one at a time: ray-traced bounce light, ray-traced reflections, ambient occlusion, then the distance of the detail (graphics-bound), or fewer officers, smaller crowds and lighter traffic (CPU-bound). The steps come back one by one when there is room, and a step that made things slow again soon after it came back is not tried again. If a device is still too slow with everything stepped down, a **lighter graphics tier is saved as the default for next time** (only if you left Graphics on Auto). A phone or tablet that loses its graphics context for lack of memory gets a message and the game carries on when it is back. Settings → Show FPS now also shows the per-system times, the draw calls and the geometry and texture counts, so a slow spot can be found.

**Performance test** (Settings → Performance test → Run, or open the game with `?bench`): a drive of about a minute in the car, steered by an autopilot along the same route every time: a downtown avenue, a bridge, the diagonal boulevard, the spiral road up a hill and a street with performers (`src/bench.js`). Every frame is timed. At the end it shows the frame rate, the slowest 1% of frames, the worst frame and the number of stutters (frames over 50 ms), place by place, with a Copy button, so a slow spot on a real iPad can be found and the numbers pasted back. The car goes through traffic and walls during the test (so a crash cannot change what is measured); everything else is on, and the result says whether the game eased its own resolution or effects while it ran. The last result is kept in Settings.

**How the city is streamed** (measured, with the CPU slowed 4 times to stand in for a slower device: `tests/map.mjs` checks the logic, the numbers below were measured by hand): a region of the city (360 m square) is built a few milliseconds at a time, between frames, and handed to the scene the same way. Before this, one step could take 25 to 30 ms at 4× slowdown (a visible stutter every time a new region was needed); now no step is longer than about 16 ms there (about 4 ms on the machine used for testing), and the whole of a region takes 30 to 140 ms in total at 4× slowdown. Building in slices gives exactly the same region as building it all at once (tested). Two bugs found on the way are fixed: a region given up half-built left its colliders in the street as invisible walls, and jumping to a place while it was being built could put the same region in the scene twice.

- **Sharp:** facade textures are drawn at about 64 pixels per metre, with window frames, sills, mullions and brickwork you can see up close; glass is shiny and metallic and reflects the sky; windows sit back in the wall (relief maps); there's grime at the foot of walls; ground textures don't visibly repeat; a sharpening pass adds crispness; and the render scale setting (75% to 150%) lets you trade speed for a supersampled image.
- **Atmosphere:** distance fades through a haze that thins with height (valleys are misty, peaks are clear), turns cooler and bluer away from the sun, and glows warm and bright when you look towards it, so the world layers up into depth like film footage.
- **Ground detail:** sand, gravel, snow and grass have fine grit and relief that catches the low sun, and snow has a faint sheen. Cliffs are textured from the side. Roads are glossy enough to streak with the sun. Close to the car, a field of grass tufts sways in the wind, dense on grassland and sparse and dry in the desert.
- **Contact shading:** on Ultra and High, screen-space ambient occlusion darkens the creases where tyres meet the road, kerbs, building bases, trunks and rocks meet the ground. A clarity pass adds local contrast, and there's a faint lens fringe towards the corners.
- **Alive:** trees, palms and bushes sway in the wind (each one out of step with the next, bending more towards the top, with the leaves fluttering), the clouds drift across the sky, hydrants spout water and lamp posts spark when they are smashed, pedestrians fill the pavements, and the traffic and police beams light the road ahead.
- **Not blocky:** towers are round, chamfered or rounded rather than boxes, with smooth shading round the curves; balconies are thin slabs with real railings; trees have leafy crowns; the street corners are rounded.
- **Lighting:** a physically based sky, real-time reflections on the car, cascaded shadows, bloom, a speed blur and a colour grade for each time of day. The resolution drops a little by itself if the frame rate falls, and rises again.

## Realism

- **A real car.** The hero car is a detailed Ferrari 458 Italia model (about 140,000 triangles on tablets and lower settings, 360,000 on Ultra) with its own interior: a leather cabin, a steering wheel that turns with yours in the driver view, mirrors, vents and dials, brake discs behind the spokes, clear-coated metallic paint in nine colours that reflects the street in real time, glass you can see through, and rubber, chrome and carbon-fibre materials. The model is by vicent091036 on Sketchfab (CC BY, via the three.js examples); see `assets/CREDITS.md`. The same car, cut down to about 16,000 triangles, also drives about in the traffic (roughly one car in ten). The police grand tourer and the rest of the traffic are still built in code.
- **Real light.** Everything is lit and reflected by a photographic HDR sky (Poly Haven, CC0), turned so its sun sits where the game's does, with the sun itself clamped (the game's own sun lamp does the sunlight and the shadows). So glass, paint, wet roads and metal pick up real colours and horizons instead of a made-up gradient. A different picture is used at golden hour, midday, sunset and night.
- **A film-like picture.** AgX tone mapping (soft, less saturated highlights than the usual game "ACES" look), gentler sharpening and colour fringing, and a grade that keeps colours natural.
- **Materials.** Glass is a neutral, dark, mirror-like reflective surface (no painted-on sky), asphalt is dark, fine and dry-rough (wet when it rains or at night), leaves are a detailed hand-painted leaf spray with veins and glow when the sun shines through them, skin has a soft sheen and pores, fabric has a weave, hair has strands, and the traffic and police have clear-coat paint.
- **Real people.** Your character is one of six photo-textured human models (an agent in a black suit, and street, denim, hoodie, leather-jacket and tee-shirt looks, men and women) from the **Microsoft Rocketbox** avatar library (MIT licence), the police officers are four more, and the people on the pavements near you are eight more. **They move with recorded human motion** (`assets/people/anims.json`, also from Rocketbox: a breathing idle, a stroll, a walk, a jog, a run and a sprint, blended by speed and run at the stride that matches the speed so the feet do not slide), with relaxed hands, **blinking and moving eyes**, and the feet kept out of slopes and kerbs. The old stick-skeleton animation (retargeted onto the real bones) still takes over where there is no recording: crouching, jumping, falling, being knocked down, and aiming a gun with both hands (IK, with a trigger finger lying along the guard).

## Ray tracing and physically based rendering

Browsers have no hardware ray tracing (neither WebGL nor WebGPU can reach a graphics card's ray-tracing cores), so the rays here are marched through the picture the game has already drawn ("screen-space ray tracing"). It runs on any GPU, including a tablet's. Switch it off in Settings → Ray tracing (it needs a reload); it is off on the Low tier.

- **Ray-traced reflections** (`src/ssr.js`). After each frame the scene's colour (still in linear HDR) and each pixel's distance from the camera are kept, with mip levels. A glossy surface (wet road, window glass, car paint and glass, chrome, water) then fires a ray along its true mirror direction, using its own normal and roughness, steps along it through last frame's picture, refines where it meets something, and reflects what it finds there, blurred by the surface's roughness. So a wet street shows the buildings, palms, lamp posts, lit windows and the cars themselves, and the paint reflects the road. A ray that finds nothing (it leaves the screen, or flies into the sky) falls back to the photographic sky lighting, so nothing pops.
- **Ray-traced bounced light** (`src/gi.js`). Each pixel fires a few rays over its hemisphere, marches them through the depth buffer and collects the lit colour of what they hit; a depth-aware blur merges 16 different ray sets per pixel. The result is added to the picture, tinted by the surface's own colour, so a sunlit road lights the underside of an awning, a car tints the tarmac beside it and green verges warm the pavement.
- **Puddles.** In rain and at night the road has patches of standing water: still-water smooth (so the reflections are sharp and mirror-like there), darker, without grain. The rest of the road is damp. The patches come from noise in world space, so they are the same each time you pass.
- **Physically based materials.** Everything uses the metal/rough model (with clear-coat paint, sheen on skin and fabric, a roughness-aware specular antialiasing, and energy-conserving image-based lighting from the HDR sky), real-world-style roughness for each surface, and tone mapping that behaves like film.

What it can't do, because it only knows what was on screen last frame: it can't reflect anything behind the camera or off the edge of the picture (those reflections fade to the sky), it can't bounce light from things you can't see, moving objects reflect one frame late, and there are no ray-traced shadows. Thin things (wires, palm fronds) in a reflection can break up a little. This is a convincing approximation of ray tracing, not a path tracer.

## Honest notes

- **The places outside the city and the new roads were only seen in a desktop browser with software drawing** (aerial and street-level pictures of every place, and the tests), not on an iPad. They are built one at a time behind the loading screen (about 0.3 s in all on the test machine; more on a tablet) and taken out of the scene altogether (so none of their textures or buffers are on the graphics chip) when you are more than 2 to 3 km away, with their shaders compiled in advance; all their sign boards share one texture page (8 MB; as 37 separate textures they were 18 MB, and a tablet short of graphics memory shows a black 3D view with the HUD still working, which is what one iPad screenshot looked like); the Performance test lists the longest stutters, which is how to find out whether the new shaders (the towns' materials, the resort's snow, the telescope dishes) stall the first time they are seen. Out there nothing moves except the windmills, pumpjacks, dishes, the flare and the lighthouse beam: no people, no traffic. The lanes and rings of the Old Town cross each other without lights.
- **Not measured on an iPad or a real GPU.** Everything was tested in headless Chrome with software rendering (far slower than a real graphics card), so frame rates on any real hardware are unmeasured: that is what the Performance test above is for, and it needs running on the iPad. What was measured here (counts that do not depend on the graphics card; Medium tier, the iPad's default, ray tracing off): an ordinary frame is about 400 to 525 draw calls and 1.3 to 2.0 million triangles (the shadow pass included; about 60% of it is trees and bushes, each car is 2,000 triangles), and every third frame also redraws one face of the car's reflection probe, which adds 0.8 to 1.5 million (so the frame time will be uneven if the iPad is struggling: a candidate for the next saving). The hills and the diagonal roads added 6% to 12% more triangles and 4% to 13% more draw calls compared with the flat city. The game's own JavaScript while driving the test route took about 0.6 to 0.7 ms a frame on the test machine, and with its CPU slowed 4 times to stand in for a slower device 3 to 5 ms on average (95% of frames under 14 ms; the rare long frames, 30 to 180 ms, are things made for the first time: a performer, the officers, a new region). The JavaScript memory stayed at 30 to 70 MB while streaming across the map. **Not measurable here, and the likeliest cause of a stutter on the iPad: the first time a new shader is used** (the river, a bridge, a diagonal road, a performer) the graphics driver compiles it, which software rendering cannot show; the Performance test lists the longest stutters by place, which is how to find them. What protects a slow device is the performance guard above and safe defaults; whether that is enough on an older iPad is for you to find out.
- **"Like GTA 6" is a direction, not an arrival.** GTA 6 is the work of hundreds of artists on a console-class GPU, with scanned assets, hardware ray tracing and a real-time global illumination system; this is a browser game with generated models that has to run on an iPad. What this pass did was add the things that make that kind of picture read as film (shaded clouds, depth of field, split-toned colour, anamorphic streaks, dust in the light, weathering on every surface, rain rings, flowers) and keep every one of them cheap and switchable by tier. **None of it was seen on a real GPU or an iPad**: the pictures were taken with software rendering (so they look right, but the frame rate is unmeasured), and the performance guard can turn depth of field off first if a device struggles.
- **Unreal Engine 5 is not in this web game, and cannot be** (it is a native engine and this runs in a browser on an iPad), so there is a **separate Unreal project in `unreal/`** (see its README; it has not been compiled or run). Inside the web game what was done instead is the same *ideas*: the world was already **streamed in cells** (like World Partition: only what is near is built, in slices that never stall a frame); cars have **levels of detail** (the point of Nanite: detail where you can see it, cheap where you cannot); the materials are **physically based** (metal, clear coat, rough rubber, real glass); the reflections are **screen-space ray tracing** (not hardware ray tracing: no RTX, no path tracing, no Lumen); wheels, steering, strobes and the like are the **micro-animations**; and the police car is built after the real thing (a US-style black and white interceptor saloon with the usual equipment). Hardware ray tracing, virtualised geometry, Lumen and Nanite meshes are not possible in the browser.
- **The fighters were tested by a simulated player** (stepping the game: the big man charging and landing blows, the armed man drawing and shooting, being knocked out or shot, the wallet, your own knock-out) and looked at in pictures (the muscle build next to an ordinary man, an armed man with his pistol up), but **nobody has fought them**: how hard they hit, how often they shoot and how many a street holds were set by guesses, so they may be too easy or too hard. The muscle build is bone scaling on the same photo-textured models, so a big man's shirt stretches with him and his texture is the pedestrian one (512 pixels), a little soft close up.
- **Robberies, rooms and melee were tested by simulated players** (stepping the game: punches landing, a clerk's hands going up, the alarm, a guard shooting, the key card, the vault, the lasers, being busted with the bag), and their pictures looked at in software-rendered screenshots, but not played on an iPad. The room is drawn on its own camera layer high above the street, which keeps the street from being drawn at all while you are inside, but the first time you enter a kind of room the device compiles its shaders (hidden under a fade, but it can still hitch on a slow tablet). The balance of what is worth what is a guess.
- **Balance is untested by people.** The numbers (officers' accuracy and damage, armour, arrest time, backup, ammunition) were set by running simulated players (standing still, jogging, shooting back) and checking the results are sensible, not by playing it. Expect to want to change some.
- **Touch, mouse and gamepad input are tested** with real touch events (several fingers at once), real mouse events and a mocked gamepad (`tests/input.mjs`), but not on real hardware: a real iPad trackpad, real fingers and a real controller can still surprise it.
- **Ray tracing** here means screen-space ray marching (see above), not hardware ray tracing. Its limits: it cannot reflect what is behind the camera or off the edge of the picture (those fade to the sky and ground probe, which shows as a dark band on a wet road close to the car), and moving things are one frame late in reflections. **On an iPad or iPhone it is off by default** (Settings → Ray tracing → Auto): a tablet showed coloured speckle over the road while it was on, which I could not reproduce or pin down without the device, so it has to be turned on there on purpose (Full, or Reflections only). Settings also shows which graphics chip, tier and effects are in use. To find a culprit on a device without touching Settings, add flags to the address: `?rt=off`, `?rt=refl`, `?gi=0`, `?ssr=0`, `?ao=0`, `?msaa=0`.
- The graphics are still mostly procedural: the buildings, trees, roads and textures are generated in code. The people, the sports car and the sky lighting are real assets (see `assets/CREDITS.md`).
- **Officers:** they use cars as cover and go round things that block them, but they still do not climb ledges or use ladders, there are no spike strips, and the cruisers' doors do not open (the officers just appear beside them). The guns on the police cars are tracers and numbers, with nothing drawn on the cars themselves. **Roadblocks** (from three stars, when you drive fast along a city street) are cruisers pulled from the pursuit and parked across the street ahead; they hold for up to 80 seconds or until you are through.
- The police chase's grand tourer is a silhouette-based recreation of a 1960s British grand tourer (no badges, logos or licensed assets), not a licensed model. The chase is the web version of the native one.
- The sounds are synthesised, not recordings. Gunfire, blasts and impacts are placed in the street (panned, softer and echoier with distance), but there are no recorded voices or sirens.
- Pedestrians are only frightened by gunfire, never hurt (a car that hits one knocks them over, which is a crime).
- The native Windows version in `native/` does not have the on-foot character, guns, officers or helicopter: those are web-only.
- Streaming the world builds geometry as you drive, so a fast drive across a biome border on a slow device can show a brief hitch or pop-in.

- `src/clouds.js`: the shader cloud layer; `src/motes.js`: dust in the air; `src/birds.js`: the birds; `src/dof.js`: depth of field; `tests/graphics.mjs`: starts the High tier and checks all of these compile, draw and are in the scene
- `src/atmos.js`: the height-aware, sun-aware haze (it replaces three.js's fog shader)
- `src/ao.js`: screen-space ambient occlusion from the depth buffer
- `src/ssr.js`: ray-traced reflections (screen-space rays through last frame's picture) and the shader patch that gives materials them
- `src/gi.js`: ray-traced bounced light (screen-space)
- `src/people.js`: the real people (loading, the stick-skeleton retargeting, recorded-motion posing, the face)
- `src/mocap.js`: the recorded motion (cycles blended by speed and phase)
- `src/seated.js`: the driver at the wheel
- `src/peds.js`: the pedestrians (instanced figures, and real people for the nearest ones)
- `src/heli.js`: the police helicopter
- `src/perf.js`: the frame-time profiler and the performance guard
- `src/bench.js`: the performance test (route, autopilot, frame statistics, the text of the result)
- `sw.js`, `precache.json`, `manifest.webmanifest`: offline play and the app icon (`node tools/gen-precache.js` rewrites the file list)
- `vendor/three/`: three.js and the add-ons the game uses
- `tests/`: Playwright smoke tests (`npm install && npx playwright install chromium && npm test`; `tests/map.mjs` covers the river, bridges, roundabouts and landmarks): start-up, no requests to other sites, offline, debug hooks hidden, menus, driving, on foot, weapons, wanted, helicopter, the performance guard, real touch, mouse and gamepad input; `tests/perf.mjs` tests the guard's decisions on made-up frame times; `tests/sites.mjs` tests the places outside the city (their ground, roads, buildings, a drive down each street, and the map's land); `tests/bigmap.mjs` tests the big map (zoom, drag, pinch, places, waypoints) and teleporting; `tests/bench.mjs` tests the performance test (its route, its numbers, and a run in the game); `tests/audio.mjs` renders the street musicians offline and checks the sound is there and not broken (`npm run audio` also writes a WAV of each performer to `out-audio/` to listen to)
- `src/weapons.js`: the guns, the arsenal, bullets, rockets and grenades
- `src/officers.js`: the police officers on foot (stepping out of cruisers, shooting, taking cover, reloading, radioing for backup, vaulting, rushing, arresting, being shot, dropping ammunition)
- `src/gadgets.js`: the guns and armour plate hidden in the cars for the police chase
- `src/grass.js`: the streamed field of grass tufts around the car
- `src/world.js`: the world: height, biomes, the motorway network and road profiles, ground and surface queries, bunkers and the airfield, the pads the places stand on and the roads to them
- `src/settlements.js`: where the towns, farms, harbour, resort and desert places are, their pads, roads and fields (plain numbers); `src/towns.js`: builds them and hides the ones that are far away; `src/kit.js`: the boxes, prisms, cylinders and beams they are made of; `src/sites-coast.js` and `src/sites-inland.js`: the harbour, village and lighthouse; the farms, resort, ghost town, oil field, solar farm and telescope
- `src/terrain.js`: the streamed terrain (a coarse whole-world mesh plus fine tiles), biome and cliff shading, and the sea
- `src/roads.js`: motorway, bridge, runway and service-road ribbons, median barriers and guardrails
- `src/scatter.js`: pines, cacti, rocks, scrub, trees and palms placed on each terrain tile
- `src/structures.js`: the bunkers, airfield, gas station, ski lodge and wind farm
- `src/tod.js`: the Golden Hour, Day and Sunset looks
- `src/textures.js`: every texture, drawn on canvases
- `src/hud.js`, `src/input.js`, `src/audio.js`, `src/fx.js`, `src/events.js`, `src/store.js`, `src/util.js`

**Debugging hooks** (`window.__game`, `__cam`, `__freeze`, `__noRender`, `__fixedRes`) exist only when the address has `?debug`.
